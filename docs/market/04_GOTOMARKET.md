## 0. Read this first — the three things that decide whether this works

1. **Your only real asset is Dr. Hatem's phone.** You have no funding, no brand, no ad budget and no sales team. In a market where clinic owners buy from people they already know, the entire first year is an exercise in converting one veterinarian's personal credibility into ten paying logos. Everything below is scaffolding around that.
2. **Your marketing site is currently working against you.** `D:\vet\marketing\index.html` (60 KB) has `<title>Aleefy — Premium Veterinary Clinic Platform</title>`, `lang="en"`, no `dir="rtl"` variant, **zero occurrences of "EGP" or "جنيه"**. The product's single loudest claim is "bilingual Arabic/English, full RTL, built for Egypt, priced in EGP" and the website is an English-only page with no prices. An Egyptian vet who lands on it concludes you are a foreign vendor. This is the cheapest, highest-leverage fix you have and it takes one weekend. It is Week 1, Task 1.
3. **The regulatory clock is your best unearned gift.** Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law No. 151/2020 had its Executive Regulations issued in **November 2025**, with a one-year grace period and **full enforcement expected 31 October 2026** — and healthcare providers, explicitly including clinics and digital-health platforms, are in scope. [V] ([Amereller](https://amereller.com/publication/the-egyptian-personal-data-protection-regime-what-you-need-to-know-ahead-of-31-october-2026/), [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/), [Baker McKenzie PDF](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/-/media/files/insight/publications/2026/01/egypt--important-data-protection-update.pdf)) You are launching three months before an enforcement deadline that makes "my client records are in a paper notebook and a WhatsApp thread" a compliance problem, not just a mess. Use it. Do not overstate it — you are not a lawyer — but it is a legitimate, dated, external reason to act now.

### (b) The 45-minute procedure to build the real group list (do this Monday)

Log in to Facebook as **Dr. Hatem** (not you — his account already has vet friends, so Facebook's group recommender will surface professional groups it will never show a stranger). Then:

1. Go to `https://www.facebook.com/search/groups/?q=` + each of these strings, one at a time:
   - `أطباء بيطريين` · `الأطباء البيطريين` · `عيادات بيطرية` · `طب بيطري` · `بيطري مصر`
   - `أطباء بيطريين مصر` · `صيادلة وأطباء بيطريين` · `خريجي طب بيطري`
   - `Egyptian vets` · `Egypt veterinary` · `vet clinic Egypt` · `small animal Egypt`
   - `طب بيطري جامعة القاهرة` (and repeat for الزقازيق، أسيوط، بنها، المنصورة، الإسكندرية، كفر الشيخ، بني سويف، قناة السويس)
2. For each result, record in a Google Sheet: **name, URL, member count, public/private, last-post date, whether the pinned rules mention إعلانات / promotion, admin name.**
3. **Discard any group whose last post is older than 14 days.** Dead groups are the most common waste of time in this channel.
4. Sort by member count × posting frequency. Keep the top 12. That is your real list.
5. Have Dr. Hatem — **not you** — request to join all 12 on the same day. Most vet groups screen entry with "are you a vet?" questions. He passes; you don't.
6. **Then do nothing for 21 days.** Read only. Note the five most-repeated complaints. Those complaints, quoted back verbatim, are your marketing copy for the next twelve months, and they are worth more than the group access itself.

**Rules of engagement once you post (this determines whether this channel works at all):**
- Almost every Egyptian professional group bans promotional posts. Assume yours do until you read the rules. Getting banned from the top group in month 2 is an unrecoverable error in a market this small.
- The move that works: **Dr. Hatem answers other people's questions for six weeks under his own name, with the clinic he works at in his bio.** No links. Then he posts one thing — not an ad, a giveaway: *"عملت ملف إكسل بيحسب استهلاك الصيدلية وتواريخ الصلاحية، مجاني لأي حد عايزه، اكتب تعليق"* — and delivers it by DM. Every DM is a lead with a name and a phone number, and you got it without breaking a single group rule.
- **Message the admin before you ever post something commercial.** Admins of Egyptian professional groups are usually reachable and often willing to allow one post a month, or to co-host something, in exchange for being asked. They ban people who don't ask.

- **GitHub Pages free tier** (fallback): 1 GB site size, 100 GB/month soft bandwidth cap, 10 builds/hour soft cap. [V] ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits))
- **Where it breaks:** the 20,000-file limit only bites if you dump a full screenshot library; Cloudflare's unlimited bandwidth makes it strictly better than GitHub Pages for a marketing site with images. **Choose Cloudflare Pages.** Netlify's free tier has a 100 GB/month bandwidth cap and is the weakest of the three for this use.
- **Non-negotiable fixes before it goes live:** an Arabic RTL version at `/ar` set as the **default** for Egyptian visitors; prices in EGP on the page (a page with no price gets no enquiry from an SMB buyer in Egypt); one WhatsApp click-to-chat button as the primary CTA (`https://wa.me/20XXXXXXXXXX?text=...` — Egyptians will WhatsApp you and will not fill in a form); Dr. Hatem's face, name and clinic, above the fold.

### CRM — **Google Sheets. Not SuiteCRM. Not EspoCRM. Not Odoo. Not Twenty.**

I am going to be blunt, because getting this wrong wastes a month. You will read about SuiteCRM, EspoCRM, Odoo Community and Twenty, install one, spend a weekend configuring pipelines, and abandon it by week five. This is the single most common self-inflicted wound for solo founders. **A one-person sales operation with fewer than 60 prospects has no CRM problem.** It has a follow-up-discipline problem, and a spreadsheet with a date column solves that.

Use one Google Sheet, these columns: `Clinic | Owner name | Phone | Governorate | Source | Status | Last contact | Next action | Next action date | Notes`. Filter on `Next action date <= today`. That is the whole system. Migrate to **EspoCRM** (self-hosted, lighter and far more maintainable than SuiteCRM) at the point where you have a second salesperson or 200+ live prospects — **not before.** Cost: 0 EGP.

### Email — **Brevo free tier**

- **Brevo free:** **300 emails/day** (~9,000/month, resets daily, does not roll over), up to **100,000 contacts** stored, no credit card, no expiry. [V] ([Brevo help](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208589409-About-Brevo-s-pricing-plans))
- **MailerLite free — significantly worse as of 1 July 2026:** cut to **250 active subscribers, 2,500 emails/month**, 2 seats, 3 automations, 3 forms, 1 landing page. Sending stops entirely once you exceed 250 contacts. [V] ([MailerLite FAQ](https://www.mailerlite.com/help/free-plan-update-faq))
- **Verdict: Brevo.** The 100k-contact allowance versus MailerLite's 250 is not close.
- **Where it breaks:** the 300/day cap is irrelevant to you — you will never have 300 Egyptian clinic emails worth writing to in year one. Brevo's paid tier starts around **$9/mo ≈ 460 EGP/mo** if you ever exceed it.
- **Business email on your own domain, free:** Cloudflare Email Routing forwards `hello@aleefy.com` to a Gmail inbox at zero cost; configure Gmail "Send mail as" to reply from the domain address. Avoid paying for Google Workspace ($7.20/user/mo ≈ **365 EGP/user/mo**) until you have staff.
- **Honest note: email is a weak channel in this market.** Egyptian clinic owners do not run their business from an inbox. Build the list because it costs nothing, but expect WhatsApp to outperform it 20:1. Do not spend time on email sequences.

### Analytics — **Umami, self-hosted**

- **Where it breaks:** self-hosted analytics dies when your VPS dies. Nobody will care. Cost: 0 EGP (shares the VPS you're paying for anyway).

- **Penpot** (self-hosted or free cloud) — only if you are designing product UI. Not needed for marketing.

### Video / demo recording — **OBS Studio + Kdenlive**

It is **90 seconds, filmed on a phone, inside a real clinic, of Dr. Hatem doing one thing** — booking a follow-up and printing an invoice — with the clinic's actual noise in the background. Polished screen recordings look like foreign software. A real Egyptian clinic on camera answers the trust objection in a way no deck can.

[V] ([Cal.com docs](https://cal.com/docs/self-hosting/license-key), [OSSAlt guide](https://ossalt.com/guides/self-host-cal-com-calendly-2026))
- **But:** self-hosting Cal.com is a Next.js + Postgres deployment you will have to maintain. Use **Cal.com's free cloud tier** for year one and self-host later if you care.

### Support / ticketing — **WhatsApp Business App (free). Not Chatwoot. Not yet.**

For ten customers, this is infrastructure you will maintain instead of selling.

Zero cost, zero maintenance, and it is the channel your customers are already using. Move to Chatwoot at ~30 customers.
- Cost: 0 EGP.

You need a permanently-running Aleefy instance that a prospect can open on their phone during a call. That is a VPS.

- **Hetzner Cloud CX** — from **€6.49–6.99/mo** after the June 2026 price adjustment ≈ **382–411 EGP/mo** [V] ([Hetzner](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud))

- **Where free breaks:** Oracle Cloud Always Free and Fly.io free tiers can host a demo, but they suspend or throttle unpredictably. A demo instance that is down when a prospect opens it costs you the deal. **Pay the 265 EGP.** This is the one line item where free is the wrong answer.

## The first 10 customers — a compressed answer

**First, a reconciliation.** `03_PRICING_AND_ECONOMICS.md` argues the honest first-year target is **5–8 clinics, not 10**, at a 40–50% design-partner discount for the first five. **That document is right and this plan does not overrule it.** The ten-customer sequence above is the *activity* plan; treat **5–8 signed at design-partner pricing as the year-one success condition**, and 10 as the stretch. Plan the cash on 5–8. Two consequences worth internalising:

- **Discount deliberately, not reactively.** The first five pay ~15,000–18,000 EGP against a 30,000 list price, and they pay it *because* they are giving you a testimonial, a site visit and a reference call. Write that exchange into the agreement so the discount buys something instead of leaking.
- **The free pilot must convert to a purchase, not a subscription.** Under a perpetual model, "3 months free then buy" is a much bigger single decision than "3 months free then 1,500/month". Mitigate it by **splitting the payment** — setup fee at signature, licence over 2–3 instalments — rather than by discounting further. Egyptian SMBs are comfortable with instalments and uncomfortable with large single transfers to an unknown vendor.

The ten do not come from ten places. They come from four, in this proportion:

| Source | Count | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Dr. Hatem's direct network** (his clinic, his classmates, his former colleagues) | **4** | The only channel that works on day one. Requires no brand, no proof, no budget — only that he is willing to spend social capital, which he must explicitly agree to before you start. |
| **Flagship + its referrals** | **2** | One visible clinic that says yes publicly is worth more than any campaign. Give it away for a year; it is a marketing cost, not a lost sale. |
| **Distributor reps** | **3** | Rented relationships at zero fixed cost. The only scalable channel available to you in year one. |
| **University / syndicate halo** | **1** | Rarely converts directly. Converts everything else by removing the "who are you" objection. Its value is indirect, and it is still worth the effort. |

The three mechanisms that actually close these, in order of importance:

1. **Do the data entry for them.** The reason clinics don't switch is not price and not features — it is the 2,000 client records in a paper notebook that nobody wants to retype. Offering *"we will type in your last 12 months, free, and you keep the notebook"* removes the only real objection, costs you time you have and money you don't, and no competitor will match it.
2. **A 3-month free pilot with a written exit.** No card, no lock-in, one page, in Arabic, stating exactly how they get their data out if they leave. The written exit is what makes the free pilot believable.
3. **Refuse to scale until it's working.** Ten customers is a hand-crafted number. If you try to automate customer 4, you will not reach customer 10.

## What will not work — say it plainly

- **Cold-calling clinics.** The phone is answered by a receptionist whose job is to book appointments; the owner-vet is in surgery. Without a referral, conversion is effectively zero and each call slightly damages your name in a market where everyone knows everyone. **Do not do this.**
- **Paid ads on any budget you currently have.** Meta ads in Egypt are cheap per impression, but your audience — *clinic owner, veterinarian, Egypt* — is perhaps 3,000–8,000 people and Meta cannot target that job title reliably in Egypt. You would pay to reach pet owners. At 0 EGP it is impossible; at 5,000 EGP/month it is waste. Revisit only when you have a case study and a retargeting audience built from your own WhatsApp list.
- **SEO.** Egyptian search volume for "برنامج إدارة عيادة بيطرية" and its variants is approximately nil. Ranking first for zero searches yields zero. Write the docs site because it costs nothing and serves customers — but do not spend one hour on keyword research, backlinks or a blog calendar in year one.
- **Posting on your own Facebook Page.** Organic reach for a business Page with no ad spend is ~1–3% of followers, and you have no followers. A person posting inside a group works; a brand posting on its own Page does not. Build the Page so you look real; do not work it.
- **LinkedIn for clinic sales.** Egyptian clinic owners are largely not there. Vet pharma reps and distributor managers are — so use LinkedIn exclusively for §1.5, and nothing else.
- **Generic international conference-mill events** (ICASVM/ICASV/ICZAS, "Cairo, 13 December 2026"). Three international conferences on the same date in the same city is a business model, not a scientific programme. No clinic owners attend. Do not pay registration.
- **Building a big email nurture sequence.** Egyptian SMB owners do not run their business from an inbox. Every hour spent on email automation is an hour not spent on WhatsApp, which converts twenty times better.

# T4 — Positioning and messaging

## 4.1 The one painful problem

Not "you need better records". Not a feature list. The switch happens when **the notebook stops being a record and starts being a hiding place.**

> **A clinic owner in Egypt does not know what his clinic earned yesterday, and cannot prove where the medicine went.**

Everything else — appointments, lab, imaging, telemedicine, AI — is a reason to stay. **Money you cannot see is the reason to switch.** In a small Egyptian clinic the assistant dispenses, the receptionist takes cash, the second-shift vet writes in the same notebook, and nothing ties a strip of medicine to a case, a case to an invoice, or an invoice to the till. The owner discovers the gap at the end of the month, as a feeling rather than a number. That is the pain that opens a wallet.

**The trigger event to watch for — this is your qualification question.** The notebook works perfectly while one person holds it. It breaks the week the clinic adds a **second vet, a second shift, or a second branch**, because two people cannot share a notebook and the owner stops being present for every transaction. **Prospect for clinics that have just grown**, not for clinics that are disorganised. A disorganised single-vet clinic will not buy. A clinic that hired its second vet last month is in active pain and knows it.

**The second story, for expansion once they're in — do not lead with it:** the clinic already has 2,000 clients and does not know which fifty of them are due for a booster this month. That is revenue it has already earned the right to and is throwing away. This is what raises the price at renewal. It is too abstract to close a first deal.

## 4.2 Arabic positioning copy — use as written

**Primary headline (H1):**

> ## اعرف عيادتك دخلت كام النهاردة — وكل شريط دوا خرج منها. من موبايلك.

**Sub-headline:**

> نظام «عليفي» لإدارة العيادات البيطرية — عربي بالكامل ومصمم للعيادة المصرية. حجوزات، ملفات المرضى، صيدلية ومخزون، فواتير وحسابات، وتذكير التطعيمات على الواتساب. شغّاله على السحابة أو على جهاز جوه عيادتك — والقرار قرارك.

**Primary CTA:**

> ### جرّبها 3 شهور مجانًا — واحنا اللي هندخّل لك بيانات سنة كاملة من الدفتر
> **من غير كارت ائتمان. تقدر تخرج في أي وقت وتاخد بياناتك كلها معاك.**

**Three proof blocks (the site's second screen):**

> **١. المشكلة اللي بتوجع فعلاً**
> الدفتر بيشتغل تمام لما تكون انت لوحدك. أول ما يبقى في دكتور تاني، أو وردية تانية، أو فرع تاني — الدفتر بيبقى مكان للإخفاء مش مكان للتسجيل. مش هتعرف الصيدلية استهلكت كام، ولا الكاش اللي دخل النهاردة كام، إلا آخر الشهر — وبإحساس مش برقم.

> **٢. بالعربي، ومن اليمين للشمال، ومن غير لخبطة**
> مش ترجمة لبرنامج أجنبي. النظام اتبنى عربي من أول سطر: أسماء العملاء، أرقام التليفونات المصرية، الأسعار بالجنيه، الفواتير جاهزة للطباعة. الموظفة في الاستقبال هتشتغل عليه من غير تدريب طويل.

> **٣. بياناتك بتاعتك — ومش هتبقى رهينة عندنا**
> تقدر تشغّل «عليفي» على سيرفر جوه عيادتك، وبياناتك ما تخرجش من المكان. وفي كل الأحوال، عندك زرار واحد بينزّل لك كل بياناتك ملف إكسل، في أي وقت، من غير ما تسأل حد. جرّب الزرار ده في أول يوم — قبل ما تدفع مليم.

**Pricing-page framing line** — aligned to the perpetual-licence model in `03_PRICING_AND_ECONOMICS.md`:

> **بالجنيه المصري. تشتري النظام مرة واحدة ويبقى ملكك.**
> رخصة دائمة + دعم وتحديثات سنوية اختيارية. ولو تفضّل تدفع سنويًا بدل مرة واحدة، ده متاح كمان.

Note the deliberate wording: **«ويبقى ملكك»** — *and it becomes yours*. In a market where the buyer's deepest fear is that the vendor disappears and takes the clinic with it, a one-time purchase is not just a pricing model, it is **the strongest possible answer to the trust objection**, and it should be sold as such rather than as a payment term.

**WhatsApp opener for Dr. Hatem to send to his network — one at a time, never as a broadcast:**

> السلام عليكم يا دكتور [الاسم]، عامل إيه؟
> أنا مشارك مع مهندس في حاجة اسمها «عليفي» — نظام لإدارة العيادات البيطرية، عربي بالكامل ومعمول للعيادة المصرية تحديدًا مش مترجم.
> مش بكلمك عشان تشتري. أنا بشغّله في عيادتي دلوقتي وعايز رأيك عليه كطبيب، وعايز أعرف إيه الناقص فيه من وجهة نظرك.

## 4.3 Handling the trust objection — the part that actually decides deals

You are an unknown vendor with no track record asking a business owner to put his patient records, his stock and his cash inside your software. **Feature demos do not touch this. Only borrowed credibility and written commitments do.**

**1. "Who are you?" → Borrow trust; never claim it.**
- Dr. Hatem's name, face and clinic on the homepage, on every one-pager, on every WhatsApp message. **A vet vouching for software is a different object entirely from software with a vet in the marketing.** He must be a visible principal, not a testimonial.
- The **Cairo University teaching hospital deployment** exists for exactly this sentence, and is worth more than any paid campaign.
- **Named, callable references from day one.** Not a testimonial quote — a phone number. "Call Dr. X at Y clinic, here is his number, he's expecting calls" is the strongest sales asset in a relationship market and the cheapest to produce.

**2. "What happens to my patient data?" → Make it structural, then demonstrable.**
- **Self-hosting is your best answer and you already have it.** "You can run it on a machine inside your clinic and nothing leaves the building" is an answer no cloud SaaS competitor can give. Lead with it even though most will choose cloud — the *option* is what builds trust, not the uptake.
- **PDPL Law 151/2020**, Executive Regulations issued November 2025, one-year grace, **full enforcement expected 31 October 2026**, healthcare providers explicitly in scope. [V] Cite it accurately, dated, with the caveat that you are not giving legal advice. Do not manufacture urgency beyond what the law actually says — Egyptian professionals are cynical about scare selling and it will cost you the room.
- **Show the export button in the first ten minutes of every demo.** Click it. Open the Excel file in front of them. This single act does more for trust than an hour of architecture explanation, because it converts a promise into an observed fact.

**3. "What if you disappear?" → The Data & Continuity Guarantee.**

This is the objection that kills deals silently — the prospect never says it, he just stops replying. Answer it before it is asked, in writing, as a **one-page Arabic annex to every contract**:

1. Automatic **weekly full export** to storage the customer owns (their own Google Drive or a machine in the clinic), in **open formats (CSV/Excel), never a proprietary format**.
2. A **perpetual right to self-host**: if the company ceases operations, the customer receives a deployable copy and a licence to run it indefinitely, **placed in escrow with a named Egyptian lawyer**.
3. **A perpetual licence — you bought it, you keep it.** Under the model in `03_PRICING_AND_ECONOMICS.md` the customer owns the licence outright; annual support and updates are optional and separable. If they stop paying support, **the system keeps running**. Say this explicitly: *لو وقفت الدعم، النظام يفضل شغّال — انت اشتريته.*
4. A stated **90-day notice period** before any hosted-service termination, and no exit fee ever.

Producing this costs you one page and a lawyer's signature. **Nobody in Egyptian SMB software offers it.** Make it a headline feature rather than fine print — it converts your greatest weakness (you are two people who might vanish) into the only written guarantee in the category.

## 4.4 Is open source an asset here? A real opinion.

**Short answer: do not open-source the Aleefy ERP core. Open-source one small, genuinely useful piece as a credibility signal, and deliver the trust benefit contractually instead.**

The reasoning:

**Open source buys you almost nothing with this buyer.** Your buyer is an Egyptian clinic owner who is a veterinarian, not a developer. He will never read your code, never audit it, and cannot deploy it. Worse, in the Egyptian SMB context "مفتوح المصدر" is commonly heard as **"free, therefore cheap, therefore unsupported"** — which is precisely the opposite of the premium, we-will-be-here-in-five-years positioning you need to overcome the unknown-vendor problem. Announcing "we're open source" to this buyer is at best noise and at worst a price anchor working against you.

**It buys you three real things, all of them narrow:**
1. A clean answer to "what if you disappear" — **but a contractual escrow clause delivers that same answer without the fork risk.**
2. Credibility with **university faculties and any institutional or government buyer**, where no-lock-in is a genuine procurement criterion. Relevant to §1.4 specifically.
3. **Free labour and a talent funnel** — vet students and junior Egyptian developers contributing, which is real but slow and needs community management you do not have time for.

**The commercial risk is not "everyone steals it".** A clinic cannot operate a self-hosted ERP; there is nothing for them to steal. The risk is concentrated in exactly one actor: **an Egyptian systems integrator, or a distributor's in-house IT team, forking your code and reselling it to the same 500 clinics with a sales force you cannot match.** That is a low-probability, high-severity outcome — and the moment you get traction is precisely when its probability rises.

**Recommendation:**
- **Core ERP: source-available at most, and only to paying self-host customers under a licence that forbids resale.** Not a public repository.
- **Open-source, publicly and loudly, one useful component** — the Arabic/RTL UI component library, or the WhatsApp appointment-reminder service. It is a genuine gift to Egyptian developers, it earns you the open-source flag for credibility and hiring, it is a marketing asset, and it is worthless to a would-be competitor.
- **Sell the benefit, not the licence.** The buyer does not want open source; he wants "my data is mine and you cannot hold it hostage." The **Data & Continuity Guarantee delivers that in one page of Arabic he can actually read**, which is more persuasive to him than a GitHub link he never opens.
