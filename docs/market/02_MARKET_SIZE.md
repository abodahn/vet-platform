# 02 — Egyptian Veterinary Clinic Software: Market Size

**Prepared:** July 2026
**Scope:** Sizing the commercial opportunity for a clinic-management software product sold to veterinary practices in Egypt.
**Method:** Desk research, English and Arabic sources. Every number carries a URL. Anything not sourced is marked `[ESTIMATE — NO SOURCE]`.

---

## 0. Read this first — the honesty warnings

Three things the reader must hold in mind before any number below:

1. **There is no official register of private veterinary clinics in Egypt that is published.** Not from the General Organisation for Veterinary Services (GOVS), not from the Veterinary Syndicate, not from CAPMAS. Every clinic count in this document is either (a) a *directory scrape* — i.e. how many clinics have bothered to list themselves online — or (b) a government count of *government* veterinary units. Both are proxies. Directory scrapes undercount rural and informal practice and overcount Cairo.

2. **The companion-animal segment is much smaller than the veterinary profession.** Egypt has tens of thousands of veterinarians. The overwhelming majority work in livestock, poultry, food inspection, government service, pharma sales, or are unemployed. A software product with appointments, pet records, vaccination reminders, grooming and a retail POS is useless to all of them. The addressable count is **hundreds of clinics, not tens of thousands**.

3. **One widely-repeated figure — "4,500 veterinary clinics in Egypt" — could not be traced to a primary source.** It surfaced in an AI-generated search summary and does not appear in any Egyptian government publication I could reach. **Do not use it.** It is roughly 6–9× the count that every directory actually shows. `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE]`

### T1.4 Geographic concentration

Consistent across two independent directories:

- **Greater Cairo (Cairo + Giza) = ~58%** of all listings.
  - evcindex: (163 + 132) / 506 = **58%**
  - Yellow Pages: 420 / 719 = **58%**
- **Alexandria ≈ 20–22%** (evcindex 111/506).
- **Greater Cairo + Alexandria together ≈ 78–80%** of the listed market.
- Rentech's Maps scrape gives the same shape: Cairo 311 + Giza 258 + Alexandria 169 = 738 of 1,463 = **50%**.
- Ken Research independently names **Cairo, Alexandria and Giza as the dominant markets** for veterinary services. [Source](https://www.kenresearch.com/egypt-animal-health-and-veterinary-services-market)

**The concentration is in fact worse than 58%,** because evcindex lists the new urban communities *separately* from the Cairo and Giza governorate pages: [New Cairo alone has 44 clinics](https://www.evcindex.com/loc/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%87%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9/), on top of the 163 + 132. Meanwhile the Delta's largest city, **Mansoura, has 17 listed clinics** ([evcindex](https://www.evcindex.com/loc/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%B1%D8%A9/)) — roughly a tenth of Cairo. Adding New Cairo pushes Greater Cairo past **65%** of the listed base, and Greater Cairo + Alexandria past **85%**.

**Commercial consequence: this is a three-city product.** A salesperson on a motorbike in Cairo, Giza and Alexandria can physically reach ~80% of the entire addressable base. Delta and Upper Egypt combined are ~20% of listings and skew heavily to livestock practice. That is genuinely good news for go-to-market cost — and it is the strongest positive finding in this document.

### T1.5 Companion-animal vs livestock — sizing the split

This distinction decides the business, and Egyptian statistics do not draw it. What is available:

- **Companion-animal practice is the fastest-growing and most sought-after veterinary specialty in Egypt**, explicitly attributed to new urban communities (New Cairo, 6th October, Sheikh Zayed, New Administrative Capital) where flats have gardens and residents keep dogs and cats. Former syndicate head Dr Khalid Al-Amery: *"الحيوانات الأليفة التخصص الأكثر إقبالا نظرا للتطور الكبير الذى شهدته المجتمعات العمرانية الجديدة"* — [Youm7, Aug 2024](https://www.youm7.com/story/2024/8/23/%D9%84%D9%88-%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%89-%D8%AA%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%B3-%D8%A8%D9%83%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B7%D8%B1%D9%89-%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%8A%D8%B6%D8%B9%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%AF%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%81%D8%B1%D8%B5/6682746)
- **The same source flags that getting a private clinic licence is difficult** — a real brake on how fast the clinic count can grow.
- Livestock context for scale: ~5.1m cattle and ~5.2m sheep (2023); ~60% of livestock is rural; rural coverage is roughly **1 clinic per 5,000 head of livestock**. [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/egypt-animal-health-and-veterinary-services-market)
- Animal production is **45% of the value of Egyptian agricultural output** — [Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/story/2024/8/23/%D9%84%D9%88-%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%89-%D8%AA%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%B3-%D8%A8%D9%83%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B7%D8%B1%D9%89-%D9%86%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%8A%D8%B6%D8%B9%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%AF%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84-%D9%81%D8%B1%D8%B5/6682746) — i.e. the *money* in Egyptian veterinary medicine is overwhelmingly in livestock and poultry, while the *software opportunity* is entirely in the small companion-animal tail.

**Proxy for the split:** evcindex lists 506 clinics of which 119 self-declare as companion-animal clinics (23.5%), but that category is opt-in and clearly undercounts — the geographic distribution (58% Greater Cairo, 22% Alexandria) is itself evidence that most *listed* clinics are urban pet clinics, since livestock practice is rural. Combining: **companion-animal share of the listed/reachable clinic base ≈ 55–75%.** `[PROXY — derived, not official]`

### What this table says

- **Tiers 2 + 3 are the entire business.** Roughly **260–420 clinics** that can plausibly pay something meaningful. Everything else is either free-tier, out of scope, or a logo.
- **The buyer is always one person: the owner-vet.** There is no procurement, no IT department, no committee. That is good (short sales cycle, no RFP) and bad (no budget line, the decision competes directly with the owner's personal cash).
- **96.99% single-owner** ([Rentech scrape](https://rentechdigital.com/smartscraper/business-report-details/list-of-veterinarians-in-egypt)) means there is no enterprise motion available. Land-and-expand within an account barely exists — a tier-2 clinic will never grow into a 50-seat contract.
- **The one structurally attractive fact:** ~80% of these clinics are inside Greater Cairo and Alexandria (T1.4), so a single small field team can cover the whole addressable market in person. In a market where on-site installation and Arabic phone support are expected, geography is doing you a favour.

**What this has already done to buying behaviour — this is evidence, not opinion:**

1. **The market has settled on one-off perpetual licences.** Al-Badr sells everything for EGP 4,999 forever and charges EGP 500/*year* for cloud backup. The entire kkctgroup cashier price list is one-off. Pharmacy software is explicitly sold as a *"رخصة دائمة"* (permanent licence).
2. **Where subscriptions exist they are quoted natively in EGP, not USD-indexed.** Both Daftra and Foodics publish separate EGP price lists.
3. **Structural discounting.** Daftra's ~50% combined haircut to secure an annual commitment is the market clearing price of a recurring obligation in a currency people expect to devalue.
4. **The National Wage Council explicitly weighs "companies' capacity to bear costs"** before mandating wage rises ([Al-Masry Al-Youm](https://www.almasryalyoum.com/news/details/4244070)) — an official acknowledgement that Egyptian SME cost tolerance is the binding constraint on any recurring obligation.
5. **Compliance spend crowds out discretionary spend.** ETA e-invoicing integration alone is EGP 7,000 as a module or EGP 30,000–80,000 as an ERP add-on. That money is spent before anything optional.

> **Do not index your price to USD.** Price in EGP, revise annually, and expect to be quoted Al-Badr's EGP 4,999 as a counter-anchor in every negotiation.

## T4 — How Egyptian SMEs actually buy software

### T4.1 Purchase model: the real finding is not "perpetual vs subscription"

The expected finding was a strong perpetual-licence preference. The evidence is **more nuanced and more useful than that**:

**The dominant local convention is that vendors do not publish prices at all.** Egyptian SME software is sold demo → quote → phone call:

| Vendor | Product | Published price? |
|---|---|---|
| [DoubleClick](https://doubleclick.com.eg/home) | ERP/accounting, "25 years, 3K clients, 30K accountants" | **No** — CTA is "طلب السعر" (request a price) |
| [Al-Ameen 10](https://alameensoft-eg.com/) | Cloud accounting, ETA-compliant | **No** — "اطلب عرض سعر يناسب شركتك" |
| [DEXEF](https://dexef.com/software/dexef-cashier/) | POS/cashier | **No** — free download + booked demo |

Perpetual-licence positioning **does** exist in the Arabic-language market and reads exactly as expected — [Injaz Soft](https://injazsoft.com/): *"لا يوجد أي اشتراك شهري أو سنوي — النظام بترخيص دائم تدفع ثمنه مرة واحدة فقط… وتمتلكه مدى الحياة"* ("no monthly or annual subscription — permanent licence, pay once, own it for life"). **Caveat: Injaz's contact number is Yemeni (+967), so this is Arabic-market evidence, not proof about Egypt specifically.**

But the counter-evidence is real: [Daftra](https://www.daftra.com/hub/افضل-برنامج-محاسبة-في-مصر), the leading Arabic SME SaaS, sells pure subscription at **$9.99–$40/month**, and its own comparison of the top five accounting products in Egypt (Daftra, Odoo $9.10–13.60/mo, QuickBooks $15–100/mo, MS Dynamics BC $70–100/mo, Dafater 62–125 SAR/mo) contains **no perpetual-licence product at all**.

**Offline operation is advertised as a headline feature** by both camps — DEXEF: *"يعمل برنامج كاشير ديكسيف في وضع الأوفلاين بدون إنترنت"*; Injaz: *"النظام يعمل بالكامل بدون إنترنت (أوفلاين)، وعند توفر الاتصال يتزامن تلقائياً"*. Daftra's own comparison page names *"الحاجة إلى الاتصال بالإنترنت"* as the cloud model's weakness for Egyptian users.

Market context (**vendor market research, not survey-grade — treat as directional**):
- Egypt SaaS market ≈ **USD 639.6m (2024)**, forecast CAGR 20.6% 2025–31. [BlueWeave](https://www.blueweaveconsulting.com/report/egypt-software-as-a-service-market)
- Only ~**35% of Egyptian SMEs are familiar with cloud ERP**; >60% state a need for digital tools. [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/egypt-cloud-erp-for-smes-market) `[VENDOR CLAIM — no published methodology]`
- **No official CAPMAS or MSMEDA survey of Egyptian SME software purchasing exists.** The OECD's *SME Digitalisation in Egypt* chapter is the best remaining lead and returned HTTP 403 on automated fetch — [worth retrieving manually](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/sme-and-entrepreneurship-policy-in-egypt_195d9c43-en/full-report/sme-digitalisation-in-egypt_16206c32.html).

> **Design conclusion: sell an annual licence, invoiced and collected once, with offline-capable operation.** Not because perpetual beats subscription in principle, but because (a) annual matches the payment rails (T4.2), (b) offline is table stakes locally, and (c) the market has no habit of self-serve monthly checkout.

> **This kills the classic SaaS motion.** Unattended monthly billing is not a viable default in Egypt. Annual invoice + human collection (bank transfer, InstaPay push, or Fawry) is the realistic model. Budget for a collections function, not a Stripe integration.

Also note: **cash dominance is explicitly attributed to trust issues with digital payment plus a large unbanked population** ([PayAtlas](https://payatlas.com/countries/egypt-eg)), and in the pet sector specifically, **cash on delivery dominates and small local grocers are >50% of pet-product sales by value** ([GlobalPETS](https://globalpetindustry.com/article/north-african-pet-industry-a-fast-growing-region-with-untapped-potential/)).

## T5 — Regulatory and practical constraints

### T5.1 ETA e-invoicing / e-receipt — the wedge, and it is real

Billing a corporate farm, breeder or insurer is **B2B → e-invoice (الفاتورة الإلكترونية)**, real-time. "Compliance" sells to Egyptian SMEs where "practice management" does not.

### T5.2 Data protection — Law No. **151 of 2020** (the brief's "151/2018" is wrong)

- Egypt's PDPL is **Law No. 151 of 2020** — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=EG)
- **Executive regulations issued by MCIT Decree 816/2025, in force 2 November 2025.** — [Shand & Partners](https://www.shandpartners.com/insights/briefings/telecoms-media-technology/the-issuance-of-the-executive-regulations-of-the-data-protection-law-and-the-establishment-of-the-data-protection-centre/), [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/egypt-important-data-protection-update)

What binds a SaaS vendor:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Licence** | Legal persons acting as controller/processor need a consolidated PDPC licence. Fees graduated by record count; processor-only gets 50% reduction. Sources conflict on the fee-exempt floor (1–10,000 records per [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/overview-of-the-executive-regulations-of-the-egyptian-personal-data-protection-law/) vs 1–100,000 per Shand). | **DPO** | Mandatory for legal persons; must be in the PDPC's DPO Registry, Category A/B/C, reassessed every 3 years. |
| **Data localisation** | **No express requirement to host in Egypt** — but personal data may not be transferred, stored or processed outside Egypt **without a prior PDPC licence**, plus a destination-country adequacy assessment **and explicit data-subject consent**. | **Breach notification** | 72 hours to PDPC; affected individuals within 3 working days. |
| **Records of processing** | Mandatory secure electronic register of consent, categories, retention, security measures, subject requests. |
| **Penalties** | EGP 5m ceiling in all sources (floor reported variously as EGP 50,000 or 200,000); criminal liability possible. |

**Enforcement status: not yet live.** The PDPC exists and has published regulations, but as of the Shand briefing **the electronic licensing portal was not operational**; [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/eJournal/2026-03-05_1/en) indicates it was expected around mid-June 2026. **Exposure begins 1 November 2026.**

**Does it cover veterinary records? Yes — through the owner, not the animal.** Owner name, phone, address, national ID and payment data are squarely personal data. Animal clinical data is not itself a natural person's data, and "sensitive data" is defined by reference to human health/genetic/biometric/financial data. `[No Egyptian guidance addresses veterinary records specifically — ESTIMATE on the vet-specific application; scope definition from DLA Piper.]` The practical consequence is unchanged: the owner record triggers the full licensing, DPO, cross-border and breach obligations.

> **Architectural conclusion: host in Egypt.** Doing so removes an entire licence class, the adequacy assessment, and the explicit-consent requirement in one decision. Deadline 1 November 2026.

### T5.3 Veterinary practice regulation

**Governing law: Law No. 416 of 1954** on the practice of veterinary medicine — [full text](https://www.laweg.net/framePlain.aspx?action=ViewActivePages&Type=14&ItemID=87&NID=43341).

- **Art. 1** — practice requires registration in the veterinary physicians register at the **Ministry of Health** *and* enrolment on the **Veterinary Syndicate** roll.
- **Art. 6** — **a licensed vet may not open more than two clinics**, and must notify the Ministry of clinic location and any permanent change within one month. *(Note the commercial consequence: this is a legal ceiling on chain formation by an individual vet, which is consistent with the 96.99% single-owner observation in T1.3.)*
- **Art. 8** — the Ministry publishes and annually updates the official list of licensed vets. *(This list was not findable online.)*
- **Art. 10** — unlawful practice carries imprisonment/fine plus clinic closure. **Penalty amounts conflict across sources** (original 1954 text: ≤3 months / ≤EGP 50; [Vetogate](https://www.vetogate.com/4708997) reports up to 2 years / EGP 10,000). **Amendment status unresolved.**
- Since **Law No. 12 of 2022** (Egyptian Health Council), new graduates must complete ≥6 months mandatory internship and pass the **EVMLE** national practice exam before licensure — [EHC](https://www.ehc.gov.eg/ar/profession-practise-exam-for-veterinary-medicine)
- The **General Organization for Veterinary Services** is the supervisory body under **Ministry of Agriculture Decree 1373/2014** — [laweg](https://www.laweg.net/framePlain.aspx?action=ViewActivePages&ItemID=92930&Type=6&NID=113164)
- Clinic registration reportedly requires a notarised lease specifying use as "عيادة بيطرية", a stamped engineering drawing showing the water source, and a tax card stating the activity — [El Watan News](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/7017265) `[search-snippet level, not independently verified]`

**Medical record retention: there is no such Egyptian legal requirement.** Say it plainly. Law 416/1954 contains no records provisions whatsoever, and nothing in the EHC or GOVS material addresses clinical records. What *does* apply is the generic tax rule: **books and documents kept 5 years** ([VATCompliance](https://vatcompliance.co/guides/vat/egypt/)); e-invoice archiving cited as 5 years by [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/02/23/briefing-document-podcast-e-invoicing-e-reporting-in-egypt/) and 7 years by [ClearTax](https://www.cleartax.com/eg/en/e-invoicing-egypt) — **design for 7.** Retention is a *billing-data* problem, not a clinical-records problem.

**Controlled drugs: a genuine regulatory hole, not a requirement.** A Youm7 investigation quotes Dr Youssef El-Abd of the Veterinary Syndicate saying ketamine and xylazine are *"أدوية مخدرة طبقا للممارسة العملية… ولكنها غير مدرجة فى جدول المخدرات"* — narcotic in practice but **not on the narcotics schedule** — and that the 1954 law *"لم يتضمن أى عقوبات أو تنظيم"*. The article documents a jurisdictional gap between the Central Pharmacy Administration, GOVS and the Pharmacy Inspection Department. [Youm7, 2019](https://www.youm7.com/story/2019/7/17/4337528) **No legal requirement for a paper narcotics register (دفتر مخدرات) in an Egyptian vet clinic could be sourced.** The source is from 2019 and the position may have changed. `[NOT SOURCED — verify with counsel before building or omitting this feature]`

**Rabies recording, pet registration, microchipping: not established.** No Egyptian source imposes these on clinics or owners. A draft law on possession of dangerous animals and keeping of dogs has been reported but its content and enactment status are unverified. Treat vaccination certificates and microchip fields as **commercially useful, not legally mandated**.

**Notifiable disease reporting:** Agriculture Law 53/1966 and GOVS surveillance exist, and WOAH listed-disease reporting is a **state** obligation discharged via GOVS — **no statutory reporting duty on private small-animal clinics with a defined disease list or channel could be sourced.** Build it only if customers ask.

### T5.4 Selling software in Egypt — tax and setup

- **VAT: standard rate 14%**, applies to software and SaaS as a taxable service; test is where the service is consumed — [Andersen Egypt](https://eg.andersen.com/tax-digital-services-in-egypt/), [Anrok](https://www.anrok.com/vat-software-digital-services/egypt)
- **VAT registration threshold: EGP 500,000** annual taxable supplies; register within 30 days of crossing — [VATCompliance](https://vatcompliance.co/guides/vat/egypt/). **Law 157/2025 amended VAT Law 67/2016 but did not change the threshold.**
- **Rate risk:** one source states professional consultancy services attract **10% schedule tax** rather than 14% ([VATCompliance](https://vatcompliance.co/guides/vat/egypt/)). **How the contract is characterised — software licence/SaaS (14%) vs consultancy (10%) — changes the rate. Get local advice.**
- **Withholding tax:** Egyptian resident customers withhold on payments over EGP 300 — **3.0% for services**, 1.0% contracting/supply, 5.0% commissions ([Andersen](https://eg.andersen.com/withholding-taxes-in-egypt/)). **Expect corporate clinic customers to withhold 3% from your invoices.** A conflicting table (0.5%/2%/5%) appears in [PwC summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/withholding-taxes) — confirm before pricing.
- **Company setup:** legal form → trade name → **GAFI** → **commercial register + tax card** → **ITIDA registration** as a technology service provider → activity licences ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/it-and-internet/1805790/start-an-it-company-in-egypt-2025-|-gafi-itida-legal-guide)). ITIDA (Law 15/2004) licenses 8 IT activity categories including software development; **fees and validity are not published** and mandatory-vs-recommended status is genuinely ambiguous in the sources — [ITIDA](https://itida.gov.eg/English/pages/information-technology-activities-licenses.aspx)
- **Foreign-currency pricing:** no rule found prohibiting USD/EUR pricing; the ETA e-invoice schema carries currency and FX-rate fields and VAT settles in EGP. `[ESTIMATE — NO SOURCE]` that Egypt's periodic FX shortages make EGP pricing with periodic revision commercially safer — that is judgement, not law.
- **Unresolved and worth checking with counsel: whether veterinary services are themselves VAT-exempt as medical services.** No source found. This determines whether your product must compute VAT on clinical line items at all.

### What would actually change the answer

Each of these is a lever the base case does not include:

1. **Gulf pricing.** [MdadTech](https://www.mdadtech.com/plans) sells Saudi clinic management at **SAR 7,000–12,000/year** — roughly **EGP 95,000–162,000/year equivalent, 5–9× Egyptian pricing** for the same product category. 2. **Expand the unit count beyond clinics.** Pet shops and superstores are **>40% of pet-care sales in Egypt** ([Euromonitor via search index](https://www.euromonitor.com/pet-care-in-egypt/report)); groomers, boarding and pet pharmacies all need the same POS + client-record spine. This is the difference between 350 addressable units and perhaps 1,500.
3. **ETA e-receipt compliance as the wedge (T5.1).** Compliance converts "nice to have" into "must have". Note the price signal: ETA integration is sold as a **EGP 7,000 one-off module** in the accounting market, and clinics have been in scope since **15 March 2025**. That is a bigger single ticket than a year of your subscription.
4. **Take a cut of payments rather than charging for seats.** Paymob is 2.75% + EGP 3 ([source](https://paymob.com/en/pricing)). A clinic grossing EGP 100,000/month generates more gross margin at a 0.5% override than at EGP 800/month in licence fees — and it scales with the customer instead of fighting their cost tolerance.
5. **Fastest-growing pet market in North Africa, from a small base.** Pet-care retail more than doubled €10.5m → €23m 2021–2025, forecast €27.4m in 2026 ([GlobalPETS/Euromonitor](https://globalpetindustry.com/article/north-african-pet-industry-a-fast-growing-region-with-untapped-potential/)). If the market triples again by 2030, the clinic count and ARPU both move. **But Morocco, with a third of Egypt's population, already has a €62.5m pet market — Egypt is underpenetrated for structural income reasons, not timing reasons.**

## Honest verdict on market size

**Neither a venture business nor, on Egypt alone, a comfortable lifestyle business. It is a two-person business with a real product and a hard ceiling — and it only clears that bar if you attach one of the levers above.**

The specifics, stated plainly:

- **The honest addressable market is roughly 350 clinics who can pay EGP 500–1,500 per month.** Not 4,500 clinics. Not tens of thousands of vets. Three hundred and fifty, of which maybe 250 are one-vet-plus-a-receptionist operations whose owner is deciding between your software and a month of their assistant's salary.
- **Central three-year SOM is about EGP 1.26 million per year — roughly USD 25,000.** Being 50% wrong in the favourable direction on both clinic count *and* price still only reaches USD 56,000/year. That is the ceiling, not the target.
- **The market wants a one-off perpetual licence around EGP 5,000, because that is what Al-Badr and every Egyptian cashier vendor has trained it to expect** — and Al-Badr already sells a veterinary-supplies POS module. Your recurring-revenue thesis is fighting the local convention, the payment rails, and the currency all at once.
- **Unattended monthly billing does not work here.** Credit cards are 2.8% of adults, Meeza is debit/prepaid, and InstaPay, wallets and Fawry are all push rails. Plan for annual invoices and human collections.

**What is genuinely good:**

- **Geography.** ~85% of the addressable base is inside Greater Cairo and Alexandria. One small field team covers the entire market in person, in a market that expects exactly that.
- **No priced competitor.** Every Egyptian and Arab veterinary system is quote-only. Publishing a clear EGP price is a differentiator that costs nothing.
- **International products are structurally excluded.** ezyVet at $549/month is EGP 27,800 — four times a receptionist's salary. They cannot come down; you cannot be undercut from above.
- **ETA e-receipt is a dated, sourced, legally binding wedge** that has applied to clinics in Greater Cairo and Alexandria since 15 March 2025, with a free public API.

**What would make me say "don't build this":** if the plan is Egypt-only, subscription-only, seat-priced, and requires more than two people to sustain. On the numbers above that plan loses money at every parameter setting I can defend.

**What would make me say "build it":** Egypt as the design partner and proving ground — cheap to serve, dense, no priced competitor, a compliance deadline already in force — with the commercial model built from day one for (a) Gulf pricing, (b) pet shops and groomers as well as clinics, and (c) a payments take-rate rather than a seat licence. On that plan the Egyptian numbers stop having to carry the business, which is the only version of this where they don't sink it.
